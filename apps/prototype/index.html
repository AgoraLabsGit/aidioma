Lesson 3 · A1

Ser vs Estar

Say what things are — and how they are right now.

58%

The rule

Spanish has two verbs for “to be,” and they split the world in a way English doesn't. Ser is for what something fundamentally is — identity, origin, profession, the date. Estar is for how something is at the moment — states, feelings, and location.

The same adjective can change meaning with the verb: el café es frío means coffee is cold by nature (iced coffee, say); el café está frío means your coffee has gone cold.

Ser — what it is

  • Soy Miguel. identity
  • Mi hermana es doctora. profession
  • Somos de Madrid. origin
  • Es lunes. date

Estar — how it is now

  • Estoy cansado. feeling
  • El café está frío. state
  • Estamos en la ciudad. location
  • Está ocupada hoy. temporary
Quick test: could it change by tomorrow? Use estar. Would it still be true next year? Probably ser.
