# AIdioma core product criteria

## Purpose

AIdioma helps someone build usable Spanish through clear teaching and repeated, meaningful practice.
This document is the short decision filter for every page, feature, and future specification.

## What a learner should feel

- I know what I am learning and why it matters.
- Practice feels varied and worthwhile, not repetitive or random.
- Feedback helps me make the next attempt better without overwhelming me.
- My progress feels encouraging and honest.
- I can return to useful language when I need it.

## The product must do these things

1. Teach before expecting performance.
   Lessons introduce one useful capability in a deliberate sequence. Practice helps the learner use
   it again in realistic situations.

2. Make collections deep enough to revisit.
   A shipping collection contains at least 50 distinct learning units, with 100 as the goal. A unit
   remains one piece of learning even if it is practised in more than one direction.

3. Avoid needless repetition.
   A practice session works through varied material before repeating an item. Starting again should
   feel fresh, not begin with the same question every time.

4. Give direct, useful feedback.
   Feedback speaks to the learner, identifies one important thing to improve, and shows the
   correction once. It does not distract with minor style or punctuation issues unless they matter
   to the skill being practised.

5. Make progress meaningful and truthful.
   Session results are immediate and clear. Longer-term collection progress must be based on real
   learning evidence, not a temporary screen state or a made-up mastery score.

6. Keep the experience simple.
   The next useful action is obvious. Settings and controls support practice without turning it into
   a configuration task.

7. Let learners keep what matters to them.
   Learners will be able to save useful words and phrases into personal collections. Personal saved
   material and shared AIdioma content are different things.

8. Reuse good learning interactions.
   Lessons and Practice should share familiar ways to see a prompt, answer it, receive feedback,
   and understand progress. They should still make different promises: lessons teach a path;
   collections offer ongoing practice.

## Decision rule

If a proposed feature makes learning less clear, practice more repetitive, feedback more confusing,
or progress less honest, it needs to change before it belongs in AIdioma.
