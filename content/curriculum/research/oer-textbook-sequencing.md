# OER & Textbook Beginner Spanish Sequencing — Research Cross-Check

**Purpose:** Cross-check AIdioma's A1 curriculum map (12 launch lessons, ~10 vocab + 12–20 sentences each,
typed EN→ES translation + flashcards) against how established beginner Spanish courses sequence their
first ~12–20 lessons/units.

**Policy note (read first):** We consult **scope-and-sequence facts only** — the *order in which topics
are introduced*. Facts (e.g. "Vistas teaches `ser` in Lesson 1, `estar` in Lesson 2") are not
copyrightable. We do **not** copy prose, example sentences, exercises, or vocabulary lists from any
source. Where a source is openly licensed (CC-BY / CC-BY-NC-SA OER) full reuse *would* be legal, but
**our policy is original content regardless** — OER is used only as a sequencing sanity check.

Research date: 2026-07-21. Items I could not directly verify from a primary page are marked **[unverified]**.

## 1. Per-source summary

### A. COERLL — Center for OER and Language Learning (UT Austin)
- **What it is:** UT Austin's OER center. Publishes openly-licensed language materials: open textbooks,
  grammar practice, podcasts, video collections. Flagship Spanish item = **Spanish Proficiency
  Exercises** — brief video clips of native speakers from across Latin America/Spain demonstrating
  functional tasks, organized by ACTFL proficiency level (Novice → Superior) and by *communicative
  function* (e.g. "describing people," "talking about daily routine," "narrating in the past") rather
  than by a linear grammar syllabus.
- **URL:** https://coerll.utexas.edu/coerll/materials/language/spanish/ ·
  https://coerll.utexas.edu/coerll/oer/spanish-proficiency-exercises/
- **License:** **Creative Commons Attribution (CC-BY)** across COERLL materials — free to use, copy,
  adapt, redistribute with attribution. Full reuse legal; not our policy.
- **Sequencing takeaway:** COERLL is **function/proficiency-organized, not grammar-linear.** Useful as a
  reminder that "Novice-level" content clusters around greetings, self-description, family, likes,
  daily routine, and simple present — but it does not give a lesson-by-lesson grammar order. Good source
  for *communicative-goal framing* of our lessons.

### B. Language Transfer — "Complete Spanish" (The Thinking Method, Mihalis Eleftheriou)
- **What it is:** A free 90-track audio course (Track 1 = method intro). Structural / "thinking-based":
  the learner reconstructs the language from patterns rather than memorizing. Track list is public.
- **URL:** https://www.languagetransfer.org/complete-spanish · lesson notes:
  https://spoken.me/language-transfer-lesson-content · full transcript PDF (volunteer):
  languagetransfer.org / Squarespace-hosted.
- **License:** Free/donation-supported; audio free to stream and download. Content released under a
  Creative Commons non-commercial license **[unverified exact variant — treat as "free, non-commercial,
  do not reproduce transcripts"]**.
- **Distinctive sequence (verified early tracks + review corroboration):**
  - T2: **cognate bootstrapping** — English↔Spanish Latin roots; vowel sounds; verb **es** ("is").
  - T3: suffix conversions (`-ant/-ent → -e`, `-able/-ible`, `-mente` adverbs).
  - T4: **quiero** ("I want"); `-ción → -r` (noun→verb); reflexive **me**.
  - T5: **yo**; object pronouns **te/lo**.
  - T6: **intento**, silent h, **voy** ("I go").
  - T7: **voy a + infinitive** (near future), movement verbs + **a**, **algo**.
  - Notable: the word for "yes" (**sí**) isn't introduced until ~lesson 9, yet by lessons 4–5 learners
    build full sentences ("I want to explain something to you") — grammar/structure precedes vocabulary.
    Present-tense conjugation systematized, then **ser vs estar**, past/preterite, then subjunctive and
    conditional across the later two-thirds **[mid/late ordering unverified at track granularity]**.
- **Idea to steal:** front-load **high-leverage patterns** (cognate rules, `voy a + inf`, `quiero +
  inf`) so a learner can *generate* sentences before owning much vocabulary.

  https://asccc-oeri.org/first-year-spanish-resources/

  - **Libro Libre – Beginning Spanish** (Huebener) — **CC-BY-NC-SA 4.0**, 10 chapters (vocab + grammar +
    culture).
  - **Yo puedo: para empezar** (Silvaggio-Adams & Vallejo-Alegre) — **CC-BY-NC**, flipped-classroom.
  - **Tarea Libre I** (ASCCC OERI) — **CC-BY**, 1,400 interactive activities (homework bank).
  - **¡Naveguemos juntos!** — **CC-BY-NC 4.0**; **¡Bienvenidos!** — **CC-BY 4.0** (13 ch);
    **¡Chévere!** (SUNY) — **CC-BY**; **Spanish 1/2/3** (Kelly-Glasoe et al.) — **CC-BY 3.0**;
    **Nuestra comunidad latina**, **Pluma**, **¡Cultivemos!** — CC-BY-NC-SA.
  - **Reedley Spanish 1 (Beginning Spanish)** — 6 chapters; license "not declared" on LibreTexts.
- **Sequencing takeaway:** These OER textbooks follow the **conventional US university intro-Spanish
  order** (essentially the Vistas/Aventuras template below): greetings → nouns/articles/gender →
  `ser` → present `-ar` → questions → `estar` → `-er/-ir` → `tener` → family/adjectives → `ir a` →
  stem-changers → `gustar` → preterite. Chapter-level detail was available; sub-topic granularity
  varied by book. **[Libro Libre per-chapter grammar list not fully enumerated on the TOC page —
  unverified beyond "10 chapters".]**

### D. SpanishDict — grammar guide
- **What it is:** Popular reference/learning site. Grammar guide is a *reference tree*, not a paced
  course, but its "beginner" cluster is explicit.
- **URL:** https://www.spanishdict.com/guide · https://www.spanishdict.com/guide/present-tense-of-ser-estar-and-tener
- **License:** Proprietary (all rights reserved). Facts only.
- **Beginner cluster / implied order:** nouns (gender, number) → definite/indefinite articles →
  subject pronouns → **`ser`, `estar`, `tener`** (grouped together as the three core irregular
  "to be/to have" verbs) → adjectives & agreement → regular present tense → possessives → numbers →
  demonstratives. Distinctive: SpanishDict **teaches `ser`/`estar`/`tener` as one bundled unit** early.

**Madrigal's Magic Key to Spanish** (Margarita Madrigal, 1951; Penguin Random House) — verified early ch:
- Ch 1: **cognate categories** `-or`, `-al`, `-ble`, `-ic→-ico`, `-ent/-ant→-e`.
- Ch 2: more cognate transforms `-ist→-ista`, `-ous→-oso`, `-tion→-ción`.
- Ch 3: **past tense first** — `-ación` nouns → `-ar` verbs → **preté­rito** ("preparé"). Narrating the
  past very early is Madrigal's signature inversion.
- Ch 4: verb **tomar** present + past; `-ty→-dad`.
- ~Ch 20: **estar** and its uses (state/location) introduced relatively late.
- URL: archive.org/details/madrigals-magic-key-to-spanish · penguinrandomhouse.com/books/105997.
- Distinctive: **cognate bootstrapping + avoid rote memorization**; builds thousands of instantly-
  recognizable words via suffix rules before formal conjugation; teaches past tense unusually early.

**Dreaming Spanish** — levels: Superbeginner → Beginner → Intermediate → Advanced.

## 3. Convergence analysis

**Near-universal in the first ~12 lessons (teach these — the field agrees):**
1. **Greetings / self-introduction** as lesson 1 (every paced course; Vistas, Aula, Duolingo, OER).
2. **Noun gender + articles** immediately after (Vistas 1, Aula 1–2, SpanishDict 1–2, OER 1).
3. **`ser` in the present** very early (Vistas 1, Aula 1, SpanishDict, LT track 2, OER 1–2). `ser`
   before `estar` is essentially unanimous among grammar-sequenced courses.
4. **Regular present `-ar`, then `-er/-ir`** — always in that order; `-ar` first (Vistas 2→3, Aula 2,
   Madrigal 4, SpanishDict 5).
5. **`estar`** within a lesson or two of `ser` (Vistas 2, Aula 3, Duolingo 3).
6. **`tener`** as an early core irregular (Vistas 3, Aula 1, SpanishDict bundle, Duolingo 3).
7. **Adjectives + agreement** in the first third (Vistas 3, SpanishDict 4).
8. **`ir a` + infinitive** for the near future as the first "tense expansion" beyond present
   (Vistas 4, Aula 4, LT 7) — universally chosen over the true future tense for beginners.
9. **`gustar`** somewhere in lessons 4–6 (Vistas, Aula 5, OER) — everyone teaches it, nobody teaches
   it first.

**Genuine disagreements:**
- **`ser` vs `estar` — split vs together.** *Split-then-contrast* (Vistas: `ser` L1, `estar` L2,
  explicit contrast L5) vs *bundled-with-`hay`* (Aula U3 introduces `hay/estar/ser` together) vs
  *bundled-with-`tener`* (SpanishDict groups ser/estar/tener as one "to be/to have" unit). Consensus is
  *introduce separately, contrast later* — but the strong minority (Aula, SpanishDict) bundles.
- **When to introduce the preterite (past).** Late for most (Vistas L6; Aula waits and does present
  perfect at U9), but **Madrigal teaches the preterite in Chapter 3** — arguing past-tense narration is
  high-value and reachable early via `-ar` verbs. Big outlier.
- **`gustar` early or late.** Mid-sequence consensus (L4–6), but functionally "me gusta" leaks in from
  L1–2 as a fixed phrase (Vistas, Duolingo). Question: teach as chunk early, or as grammar mid.
- **Grammar-linear vs function/CI-first.** Vistas/OER/SpanishDict = grammar-linear. Aula/Duolingo =
  communicative/task themes with grammar embedded. LT = structure-before-vocabulary. Dreaming Spanish =
  no grammar sequence at all. Madrigal = cognate/suffix rules before conjugation.
- **Numbers & time.** Vistas front-loads (L1–2); most others scatter them. Low-controversy but
  placement varies.

## 4. Distinctive sequencing insights worth stealing (as IDEAS, not content)

1. **Cognate bootstrapping (Language Transfer + Madrigal).** Before any vocab grind, teach 6–9 suffix
   rules (`-tion→-ción`, `-ble→-ble`, `-ous→-oso`, `-ty→-dad`, `-ment→-mento`, `-ic→-ico`). A learner
   instantly "owns" hundreds of recognizable words. **High ROI for an EN→ES typing app** — early
   lessons can drill cognates where the learner types near-identical words and builds confidence.
2. **Generate-before-memorize / high-leverage patterns first (LT).** Teach `quiero + inf` and
   `voy a + inf` extremely early so the learner produces full, useful sentences ("I want to eat,"
   "I'm going to study") with a tiny verb inventory. Multiplies expressive range per vocab item.
3. **`ser`/`estar`/`tener` as the "power trio" (SpanishDict).** Bundling the three highest-frequency
   irregulars gives immediate sentence-building capacity — worth considering vs the strict Vistas split.
4. **Task/function framing per lesson (Aula, COERLL, Duolingo).** Anchor each lesson to a communicative
   goal ("introduce yourself," "order food," "describe your family," "say where things are") and let the
   grammar serve the goal — matches our chat-centric, sentence-translation model better than a bare
   grammar list.
5. **Early past-tense taste (Madrigal).** Even one lesson of simple `-ar` preterite ("comí / hablé")
   in the first dozen gives learners narrative power and breaks the "stuck in present tense" plateau.
   Contrarian but defensible for motivation.
6. **Comprehensibility-ordered vocab (Dreaming Spanish).** Sequence vocabulary by frequency +
   picturability, not by thematic tidiness — supports our flashcard-first + typed-translation loop.

## 5. Recommended "consensus first-12" ordering (input to our curriculum map)

Grammar-linear spine (matches Vistas/OER majority) with LT/Madrigal high-leverage accelerants folded in.
Each lesson = one communicative goal + one grammar focus + ~10 vocab.

| # | Communicative goal | Core grammar | Rationale (who agrees) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Greet, introduce yourself | subject pronouns; **ser** (soy/eres/es); gender of nouns; def/indef articles | Universal L1 (Vistas, Aula, SpanishDict, OER) |
| 2 | Recognize & type Spanish words | **cognate/suffix rules**; noun gender reinforcement | LT/Madrigal high-ROI; ideal for a typing app early win |
| 3 | Say what you do | present **-ar** verbs; forming yes/no & wh- questions | Vistas 2, Aula 2, Madrigal 4 — `-ar` always first |
| 4 | Say where you & things are | **estar** (present) + **hay**; location words | Vistas 2, Aula 3, Duolingo 3 |
| 5 | Contrast being: identity vs state | **ser vs estar** side by side; numbers 0–30 | Consensus "introduce split, contrast now" (Vistas 5, Aula 3) |
| 6 | Talk about more actions | present **-er/-ir** verbs; **tener** | Vistas 3, Aula 2/1, SpanishDict |
| 7 | Describe people & things | descriptive adjectives + agreement; possessives | Vistas 3, SpanishDict 4 |
| 8 | Talk about family | family vocab; `tener` extended; possessives reinforced | Vistas 3, Duolingo 5 |
| 9 | Say what you like | **gustar** (me/te/le gusta[n]) | Vistas/Aula 5/OER mid-sequence |
| 10 | Talk about plans | **ir** + **ir a + infinitive** (near future) | Vistas 4, Aula 4, LT 7 — the universal first tense-expansion |
| 11 | Express wants/needs & obligation | **querer + inf**, **tener que + inf**, stem-changers (e→ie/o→ue) | LT `quiero` early; Aula 4; Vistas 4 |
| 12 | Narrate a simple past event | **preterite of regular -ar (+ -er/-ir)** | Vistas 6; Madrigal-inspired early taste for motivation |

**Design rationale:**
- Keeps the **field-consensus spine** (greetings → gender/articles → `ser` → `-ar` present → `estar` →
  contrast → `-er/-ir`+`tener` → adjectives → `gustar` → `ir a` → wants → preterite) so learners who
  cross-reference any mainstream course see the same landmarks.
- **Inserts cognate bootstrapping at L2** — cheap, motivating, and uniquely suited to our EN→ES typing
  mechanic (learner types words that are 90% identical).
- **Folds `querer/ir a + inf` (L10–11)** early per Language Transfer, so learners produce real sentences
  before mastering full conjugation tables.
- **Ends the first 12 with a preterite taste (L12)** — the one deliberate Madrigal-flavored bet, to
  avoid the present-tense plateau and give narrative payoff before the A1 block ends.
- **Open decisions for curriculum-map owner:**
  1. Bundle `ser/estar/tener` (SpanishDict style) or keep the staged split above? (We chose staged.)
  2. Is L12 preterite too early for a translation-typing app, or a motivating capstone? (Flag for A/B.)
  3. Where do numbers/time/dates live — front-loaded (Vistas) or drip across lessons? (We placed 0–30
     at L5; 31+ and time/dates unplaced — decide.)
  4. `gustar` as a fixed chunk from L1 vs. formal grammar at L9 — consider teaching the chunk early,
     grammar later.
